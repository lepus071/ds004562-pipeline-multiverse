Decoding the brain, or decoding the pipeline?

One dataset, several analysis pipelines, one frozen mask. How far does a whole-brain decoding map move when the pipeline moves — and does the published verdict survive?

Agreement is Spearman rho between two group accuracy maps, voxel by voxel, inside one frozen mask of 158,156 voxels, unthresholded. The three pipelines implement the same decoding design; they differ only in the software that produced the maps.

The maps themselves

Each panel is a glass brain: the whole volume projected onto three views, so everything the map contains is visible at once. Pick a branch and a map to compare the three pipelines side by side. Tap any panel for the full volume and an interactive viewer.

Every branch tells the same story

Eleven analysis branches, three pipeline pairs. Pipelines that share preprocessing agree most; swapping preprocessing costs about a quarter of a rho; changing both costs most. The ordering never reverses.

A group map flatters every comparison

The same correlation computed inside each participant and then averaged is .13 to .19 lower than the correlation between group maps. Averaging first removes the noise that a single participant's map actually carries.

Table view

Thresholded conclusions are the fragile part

The maps above agree closely. Once a significance threshold is applied, the same three pipelines can differ by more than a hundredfold in how much of the brain they call significant — with nothing but preprocessing between two of them.

Table view

What this means

Preprocessing is the tool that matters. Swapping the downstream software barely moves the map; swapping preprocessing moves it about four times as far, and can change whether an effect survives correction at all.

Report the unthresholded map. Two pipelines can agree at .67 voxel for voxel and still disagree forty-fold about where the effect is significant. The threshold, not the pattern, is what diverges.

Group-level agreement is an optimistic number. If a comparison matters, compute it within participants as well.

A fourth pipeline is missing from this page. A second laboratory analysed the same dataset independently, with a different cross-validation scheme and a different label construction as well as different tools. Their maps and the agreement numbers that involve them are withheld until they agree to publication.

Agreement uses ranks rather than raw values, so the pipelines' different accuracy scales cannot drive it. n = 22, except where a branch needs the cohort shared with the fourth pipeline.
